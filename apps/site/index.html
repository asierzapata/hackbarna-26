How it works

A place to work something out, not a column of chat.

Most AI tools give you one scrolling conversation and ask you to hold the rest in your head. Kan puts the whole problem in front of you, and lets an agent work in the same space you are working in.

01

You can see what it did

The agent places notes, tables, charts, maps, timelines and calendars onto the board, connects them and arranges them. You watch it happen, and the part of the canvas it is working on lights up. Nothing is hidden behind a summary of what it claims to have done.

02

Bring your own agent

Kan talks to agents over the Agent Client Protocol. Devin and OpenAI Codex are supported today. Both run as a local process on your Mac under the subscription you already pay for, which is why Kan never holds a model token of yours.

03

Local by default

Boards you work on alone live on your machine, in the app's own storage. They open instantly and keep working with no internet connection. Nothing leaves your Mac until you deliberately share a board.

Rooms

Meetings that leave something behind.

Open a room and a few people share one canvas, with video and live transcription running. The board you end the call with is the thing you keep working on.

What was said is kept Speech is transcribed live and the final text becomes part of the room's conversation, next to the board it was about. No separate recording to rewatch.

How it's built

Standard parts, put together carefully.

A native shell, built with Tauri

Rust handles the system side and the agent processes; the interface renders in the Mac's own web view. It is a real desktop app, not a website in a window.

A canvas built on tldraw

Rather than write a canvas engine, we built on tldraw and added the node types and the agent tooling on top of it.

Agents over ACP

The agent reaches the board through a narrow set of canvas tools, not by driving your machine. It can read and change the canvas you have open, and nothing else.

A room server you can point at

Shared boards sync through a room server, with Vonage handling video and transcription. Solo boards never touch it.
